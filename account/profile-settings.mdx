Your Google profile is the foundation of your account identity. From the Profile Settings page, you can update your personal information, tighten your security posture, control which third-party apps have access to your data, and choose exactly how and when Google communicates with you. Taking a few minutes to review these settings ensures your account reflects who you are and stays protected.

## Personal Information

Keep your profile details accurate so Google can identify you correctly across all products and reach you when it matters.

- **Display Name** — Update the name shown across Google products. Navigate to **Profile Settings → Personal Information** and edit the **Display Name** field.
- **Profile Photo** — Upload a photo or avatar to help collaborators recognize you. Click your current photo to replace it — recommended size is 400×400 px.
- **Email Address** — Change your primary email address. Note that updating your email triggers a verification message to the new address; the change does not take effect until you confirm it.
- **Timezone** — Set your local timezone to ensure timestamps, scheduled tasks, and calendar events display correctly for your location.

Click **Save Changes** after editing any field to apply your updates immediately.

## Security Settings

Protecting your account starts with strong credentials and regular hygiene. Use the Security Settings panel to stay in control of who can access your account.

- **Change Password** — Navigate to **Security Settings → Password** and enter your current password before setting a new one. Use a unique password of at least 12 characters that you don't reuse elsewhere.
- **Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)** — Add a second layer of verification to your login. See the step-by-step guide below.
- **Active Sessions** — Review every device and location currently signed in to your account. Click **Revoke** next to any session you don't recognize to sign it out immediately.
- **Revoke Access** — Remove permissions granted to third-party applications and integrations directly from the security panel.

### Enable Two-Factor Authentication

<Steps>
  <Step title="Go to Security Settings">
    Sign in to your Google account and navigate to **Profile Settings → Security Settings**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click 'Enable Two-Factor Authentication'">
    Find the **Two-Factor Authentication** section and click the **Enable Two-Factor Authentication** button to begin setup.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose your verification method">
    Select either **Authenticator App** (recommended for stronger security) or **SMS** to receive a one-time code via text message.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Link your authenticator app or phone number">
    - **Authenticator App:** Open your preferred authenticator app (such as Google Authenticator or Authy) and scan the QR code displayed on screen.
    - **SMS:** Enter your mobile phone number and click **Send Code** to receive a verification text.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter the verification code">
    Type the six-digit code from your authenticator app or SMS message into the confirmation field and click **Verify**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save your backup codes">
    Google generates a set of one-time backup codes you can use if you lose access to your primary 2FA method. Download or print these codes and store them in a secure location — such as a password manager or a locked physical file.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Connected Applications

Google lets you grant third-party applications access to your account data via OAuth 2.0. The Connected Applications panel gives you full visibility into every app that currently holds active permissions.

For each connected app, you can see:
- The app's name and logo
- The permissions (scopes) it was granted
- The date access was first authorized

To revoke an application's access, click **Revoke Access** next to its name. This immediately invalidates the app's tokens — it will no longer be able to make API calls on your behalf until you re-authorize it. Regularly audit this list and remove any apps you no longer use or recognize.

## Notification Preferences

Control how Google keeps you informed. Navigate to **Profile Settings → Notifications** to configure your preferences across the following channels:

- **Email Notifications** — Choose which events trigger an email: security alerts, billing updates, product announcements, and activity digests. Toggle each category on or off individually.
- **Push Notifications** — If you've installed any Google mobile or desktop apps, enable push notifications to receive real-time alerts without checking your email.
- **Digest Frequency** — For non-urgent notifications, set a delivery cadence (immediate, daily digest, or weekly digest) so updates arrive when it's convenient for you.

<Note>
  Always click **Save Changes** after updating your profile or preferences — unsaved changes are discarded if you navigate away from the page. Security changes such as enabling 2FA or revoking a session take effect immediately and do not require a separate save action.
</Note>
