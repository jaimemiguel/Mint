Google offers a comprehensive suite of cloud-based products and services designed to help you communicate, collaborate, and get things done — wherever you are and at whatever scale you need. Whether you're a solo professional relying on Gmail and Google Drive, a growing team using Google Workspace to collaborate in real time, or an enterprise developer building production systems on Google Cloud, the platform is built to grow with you. This page introduces the core capabilities, common use cases, and the building blocks that make Google's ecosystem work.

## Supported Use Cases

Google's platform is flexible enough to serve a wide range of users and workflows.

### Personal Productivity

If you're an individual user, Google's tools help you stay organized, manage your schedule, and access your files from anywhere. Use Google Calendar to plan your day, Google Drive to store and share documents, and Google Keep for quick notes and reminders. Everything syncs across your devices automatically, so you're always working with the latest version of your content.

### Team Collaboration

For teams, Google Workspace brings communication and collaboration into a single, integrated environment. Your team can co-author documents in Google Docs, track projects in Google Sheets, run meetings in Google Meet, and coordinate over Google Chat — all without switching between disconnected tools. Admins can manage users, set sharing permissions, and enforce security policies from a central console.

### Developer Integrations

If you're building software, Google's APIs give you programmatic access to the same infrastructure and services that power Google's own products. Integrate Gmail into your customer support platform, read and write Google Sheets data from your application, trigger Cloud Functions in response to events, or use the Google Maps Platform to add location features to your product. The [API Reference](/api/introduction) documents every available endpoint with request and response examples.
